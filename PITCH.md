## Ways a Brown relationship could work (no particular order)

**1. Brown helps fund the initiative** — via a grant, free internal labor,
subsidized Anthropic tokens, whatever.
> Connects: the spend ledger makes the ask legible — the entire corpus to
> date (174 subjects, ~3,500 quote-confirmed citations, the whole demo that
> won Sydney over) cost roughly $50 in tokens. Small money, provable burn
> rate, obvious multiplier story.

**2. Brown as research partner** — build a closed LLM together with all the
resources we need to build the influence graph: what can't be done on the
open web alone.
> Connects: every honest yellow ⚠ chip in the corpus is this argument made
> visible — the pizza page is 93% unverifiable *because no open catalog
> exists*, dance verification barely exists, Kitsou Dubois is nearly
> invisible in anglophone sources. Brown's libraries, archives, and
> licensed collections are exactly the "closed web." The chips are the
> demo of the problem; the partnership is the solution.

**3. Brown weaves Kynda into its arts, CS, and anthropology curriculums** —
a true multidisciplinary effort.
> Connects: the media-agnostic thesis Sydney already loves IS the
> multidisciplinary claim (Beyond the Medium as a card type). Curriculum
> use also generates exactly the labor in #1 and the contributors in #4.

**4. Brown opens Kynda to the student body as first-class contributors to
the influence graph.**
> Connects: the contributor pipeline already exists end to end — Ask Kynda
> hunches → curator queue → source-backed evidence → published cards with
> the contributor's name attached. Built for one kid with an Ovid hunch;
> designed for a campus. The flywheel (every verified curiosity enriches
> the graph) is the MASTERPLAN's core loop with students as the engine.

**5. Brown lends its name and authority** — opening the door to
partnership / data-access conversations with big whales: the New York
Times, NPR, BBC, and international sources of culture data.
> Connects: interview archives are the T2 citation motherlode ("artist's
> own words" is the top of the citation-degree ladder and the scarcest).
> This is #2's sibling: #2 is scholarly closed data, #5 is journalistic
> closed data. Brown's letterhead converts cold outreach into institutional
> conversation.

**6. Brown helps us build a bridge back to Steven Johnson** — fellow alum,
who almost became a Kynda board member but then got distracted building
NotebookLM — and perhaps a bridge to Google (another whale).
> Connects: Johnson's entire oeuvre is Kynda's thesis in book form — *Where
> Good Ideas Come From* is about ideas having lineages, the adjacent
> possible, innovation as network. He didn't drift to something unrelated:
> NotebookLM is source-grounded AI — the same religion as quote-confirmed
> citations, practiced at Google scale. The re-approach pitch writes
> itself: "the influence graph is the book you kept writing." Google as
> whale: YouTube (performance footage — half our curated Experience-it
> links), Google Books, and Arts & Culture (already a default door on art
> cards) are the distribution/data trifecta. Note this is a SIXTH distinct
> mechanism: Brown as alumni network hub, not Brown as institution.

## Observations across the five (Kynda's notes, not Tony's)

- The five aren't parallel options — they compound: 3 feeds 4, 4 feeds the
  graph, the graph's gaps justify 2 and 5, and 1 underwrites the loop.
  A pitch structure could present them as one flywheel rather than a menu.
- Sydney's own world (choreorobotics, CRCI) sits squarely in the #2
  argument: dance is the least-cataloged major art form — the domain where
  a closed-corpus partnership pays off first and most visibly.
